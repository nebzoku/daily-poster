# Illustration style guide (the "Style Describer" step)

Written from Ben's reference set (26 Nov 2026; other artists' work, deliberately not kept in the repo). This text, plus
images of our own illustrations that worked (`lab/illo-guide/`), is what the illustration machine
(`scripts/illo-gen.mjs`) sends to the image model. It describes a style, never a particular artist, character or picture.

## Style description
Contemporary editorial illustration in clean ink line and flat colour. Even, fine black or very dark contour lines
(slightly worn, with small breaks where the pen lifts) describe every form; colour sits in flat areas inside them, with
one cel-shadow tone per surface and no soft gradients except skies and glows, which are stepped bands. Compositions are
architectural and cinematic: strong one- or two-point perspective, dramatic camera angles (very low looking up, very
high looking down, slight fish-eye at the edges), big simple spaces against small, precise detail. Surfaces carry
thin-line texture: short hatched blades of grass, ripples on water, bark grooves, brick courses, floorboards. Figures
are naturally proportioned and drawn with confident outline, simple faces, clearly articulated hands with all fingers.
Light is directional and graphic: hard-edged shadow shapes, pools of light, sun through windows. Mood is calm, warm and
observant, often quietly humorous.

## Key style tags
editorial illustration, clean ink line, flat colour, cel shading, one shadow tone, stepped sky gradient, dramatic
perspective, low angle, high angle, fish-eye, architectural detail, thin-line hatching, hard shadows, limited palette,
one accent colour, risograph print feel, paper grain

## Colour
A limited palette chosen per day: the day's four colours (and their tints and shades) for everything, plus black for
line, plus ONE highlight colour used on a single small focal element. Whole scenes may sit in one hue family.

## Composition rules for posters
- Leave the headline's area calm: flat, solid colour with no line detail where the headline will sit (the prompt names
  the region: e.g. "the top 20% of the image is plain sky").
- Portrait A-size (about 1:1.41). The subject is relevant to the day's story at a glance.
- Hands, fingers and faces must be anatomically right: five fingers, natural knuckles, the thumb its true length.
- Faces (Ben, 26 Nov: the close-up pillion pair looked "cheesy and AI generated"): no grinning, posed, glossy faces
  looking at the viewer. Keep people at a distance, seen from behind or in profile, faces small and simply drawn (a
  few lines, no shine, no airbrushed skin), absorbed in what they're doing. Never a stock-photo smile.

## Faces (Ben, 26 Nov: generic faces are what make it look AI and less ownable)
Every face is a specific person, not a template. Drawn with very few lines: small dot or short-dash eyes, a single
line for the nose (often just its edge in profile), a short mouth line, no eyelashes, no lips, no shine, no rendered
cheeks or airbrushed skin, flat skin colour with at most one hard shadow shape. Mostly profile or three-quarter, absorbed
in something, rarely looking at us; at most a slight, wry expression, never a toothy smile. What makes each one
individual is the character detail: a distinctive haircut or wrap, glasses, a hard hat, earrings, a moustache, a
heavy brow, an unusual nose or chin, age lines, a slightly odd proportion. No two faces in a scene share the same face,
build or hairstyle.

## Cast (Ben, 26 Nov)
Whenever there are people, cast them diversely and true to the story's place: a range of skin tones, ages, body types,
genders and dress, never defaulting to a uniformly Western, white, young, slim crowd. If the story is set somewhere
specific (Swat in Pakistan, Appalachia, Gloucestershire), the people look like the people who live there, with the
clothes, hair and head coverings they'd actually wear.
